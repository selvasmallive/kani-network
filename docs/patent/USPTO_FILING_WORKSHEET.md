# USPTO Provisional Filing Worksheet

This worksheet is for organizing filing information for counsel. It is not legal advice and is not an official USPTO form.

## USPTO Basics To Confirm With Counsel

The USPTO describes a provisional application as a lower-cost first filing that can establish a U.S. filing date for an invention, but it is not examined and is automatically abandoned 12 months after filing. To benefit from the provisional filing date, a corresponding nonprovisional must be filed within the 12-month pendency period.

The USPTO states that a complete provisional application includes a written description of the invention, drawings if necessary, the filing fee, and a cover sheet or Application Data Sheet identifying the application as provisional. Formal claims, an oath/declaration, and an information disclosure statement are not required for a provisional application.

USPTO source pages for counsel:

```text
https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/types-patent-applications
https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/provisional-application
https://www.uspto.gov/patents/apply/forms
https://www.uspto.gov/patents/apply/patent-center
```

The USPTO notes that a public disclosure by an inventor may be relevant to the U.S. one-year grace period and may affect foreign patent rights. Have counsel evaluate this before any further public disclosure.

## Items Counsel Should Decide

- Whether to file one broad provisional or split into multiple provisionals.
- Whether to add formal claims now even though they are not required.
- Whether any GitHub or cloud evidence should be omitted or generalized.
- Whether crypto-agility, budget guardrails, ISO translation, and GKE validator operations should be claimed together or separately.
- Whether foreign filing is desired and whether any public disclosure affects foreign rights.
- Whether trademark strategy for `KANI` should be handled separately.
